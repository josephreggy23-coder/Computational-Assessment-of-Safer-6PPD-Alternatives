# Impact framework

## Impact pathway

```text
Public measurements
        |
        v
Comparable parent/product evidence
        |
        v
Testing-priority decisions
        |
        v
Earlier rejection of harmful substitutes
        |
        v
Lower risk to salmon-bearing watersheds
```

## Primary impact metric

The primary metric is not model accuracy. It is:

> the number of official alternatives for which the framework can identify the
> single most decision-changing missing measurement.

Examples include:

- no transformation-product identification;
- no coho or salmonid toxicity measurement;
- no measured environmental persistence value;
- no unambiguous molecular identity;
- no tire-performance evidence.

## Secondary impact metrics

- Fraction of official candidates with organism-level toxicity evidence.
- Fraction with ozonated-product evidence.
- Fraction with a resolvable chemical structure.
- Number of candidates incorrectly appearing reassuring when only parent
  toxicity is considered.
- Number of candidates that remain unranked rather than receiving an
  unsupported score.

## Stakeholder outputs

### Regulators

A concise testing-priority table with source links and explicit uncertainty.

### Tire and materials researchers

A shortlist of mechanistically distinct candidates needing comparable ozonation
and performance experiments.

### Watershed and tribal communities

A plain-language impact brief explaining what is known, what remains unknown,
and why "less toxic parent chemical" does not necessarily mean safer runoff.

### Scientific judges

A reproducible chain from raw measurement to conclusion, including negative
results and limitations.

## Guardrails

- No candidate is called safe from computational evidence alone.
- Proprietary mixtures are not assigned fabricated structures.
- Predicted properties are not relabeled as measurements.
- Cell-line endpoints are not equated with whole-fish lethality.
- Missing data do not improve a candidate's ranking.
